## What's new

- **Much faster old 32-bit games**: x87sidecar (v1.9.1, MIT, built from source) now runs the old x87 floating-point
  math of 32-bit games as native Apple silicon code instead of through Rosetta's slow path. AnvilMac turns it on by
  itself for 32-bit games when it supports your Mac's Rosetta; Game Settings can turn it off for one game.
- **A per-frame timing log for Direct3D 10 and 11 games** (off unless asked for), which AnvilMac's tests use to
  measure stutter.
- Everything from 11.19-14, including msync and the frame rate for Direct3D 10 and 11 games.

## Measured

- A 32-bit test doing x87 math on an M2 Pro: 22.4 s without x87sidecar, 0.76 s with it (29.5 times faster), same
  results.
- Played by the owner with the same changes: Max Payne from a median of 35 to 120 fps (its slowest moments from 15 to
  91 fps); Shank's slowest moments from 38 to 57 fps (held 60 fps); SPORE's dips under its 30 fps cap gone.
- Direct3D 10 and 11 games: unchanged (a hidden test runs at about 120 fps, as on 11.19-14).

## Known problems

- Bink videos in the ReXGlue ports (Naruto) stay slow. That is an issue in the ports and has been reported to them.
- x87sidecar does x87 math with 64-bit instead of 80-bit precision. If a 32-bit game behaves differently, turn
  "Faster math for older 32-bit games" off in its Game Settings.
